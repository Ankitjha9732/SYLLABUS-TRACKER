# GATE CS 2027 — Detailed Preparation Plan
**Duration:** September 2026 – February 2027 (~23 weeks)
**Background:** Final-year BTech CSE student, GATE CS & IT paper
**Aligned to:** Official GATE 2027 CS Syllabus (IIT Madras)

---

## How to use this plan
- Each week has a subject focus, topics to cover, and a practice target.
- Solve **Previous Year Questions (PYQs)** for every topic right after you study it — don't wait till the end.
- Maintain **one running notebook** of formulas, tricky concepts, and mistakes — this becomes your revision bible in January.
- Take a **topic-wise test** at the end of every subject, not just full mocks later.

---

## PHASE 1: Foundation Subjects (Sept 1 – Sept 30) — 4 weeks

### Week 1: Digital Logic (Section 2)
- Boolean algebra & minimization — algebraic technique, K-maps, tabular (Quine-McCluskey) method
- Design of combinational circuits (adders, multiplexers, decoders)
- Design of sequential circuits (flip-flops, counters, registers)
- Number representation and arithmetic — fixed point and **floating point**
- **Practice:** 25 PYQs by end of week
- **Resource:** Morris Mano (Digital Design) — relevant chapters only

### Week 2: Programming & Data Structures (Part 1) (Section 4)
- Programming in C, recursion
- Arrays, stacks, queues, linked lists
- **Practice:** Code/trace 15 problems on recursion + linked lists

### Week 3: Programming & Data Structures (Part 2) (Section 4)
- Trees, binary search trees, binary heaps
- Graphs — representations, traversals (BFS/DFS)
- **Practice:** 30 PYQs across DS topics
- **Resource:** Narasimha Karumanchi (Data Structures) or standard GATE DS notes

### Week 4: Engineering Mathematics — Discrete Math (Section 1)
- Propositional and first-order logic
- Sets, relations, functions, partial orders and **lattices**
- **Monoids, Groups**
- Graphs: connectivity, matching, colouring
- Combinatorics: counting, recurrence relations, generating functions
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs
- **Daily:** Start 15-20 min General Aptitude practice from this week onward (continue till exam)

---

## PHASE 2: Core Heavy Subjects (Oct 1 – Oct 31) — 4 weeks

### Week 5: Computer Organization & Architecture (Part 1) (Section 3)
- Instruction set and addressing modes
- Design of ALU
- Design of control unit — hardwired and microprogrammed
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs

### Week 6: Computer Organization & Architecture (Part 2) (Section 3)
- Instruction pipelining, pipeline hazards
- Memory interfacing and hierarchy: performance, cache memory mapping
- I/O interface (interrupt and DMA)
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs + 1 topic test (Weeks 5+6 combined)

### Week 7: Algorithms (Part 1) (Section 5)
- Asymptotic worst-case time and space complexity, recurrence relations (Master theorem)
- Searching, sorting, **hashing**
- **Practice:** 25 PYQs

### Week 8: Algorithms (Part 2) (Section 5)
- Algorithm design techniques: greedy, dynamic programming, divide-and-conquer
- Graph traversals, minimum spanning trees, shortest paths
- **Practice:** 25 PYQs + 1 topic test
- **Resource:** CLRS (selective chapters) or standard GATE algorithms notes

---

## PHASE 3: High-Weight Subjects (Nov 1 – Nov 30) — 4 weeks

### Week 9: Operating Systems (Part 1) (Section 8)
- System calls, processes, threads, inter-process communication
- Concurrency and synchronization
- CPU scheduling
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs

### Week 10: Operating Systems (Part 2) (Section 8)
- Deadlock
- Memory management and virtual memory
- I/O scheduling, **file systems**
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs + 1 topic test

### Week 11: Databases (Part 1) (Section 9)
- ER model, relational model: relational algebra, tuple calculus, SQL
- Integrity constraints, normal forms
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs

### Week 12: Databases (Part 2) (Section 9)
- File organization, indexing (B and B+ trees)
- Transactions and concurrency control
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs + 1 topic test
- **Resource:** Navathe or Korth (selective chapters)

---

## PHASE 4: Remaining Core + First Mocks (Dec 1 – Dec 31) — 4-5 weeks

### Week 13: Computer Networks (Part 1) (Section 10)
- Principles of layering
- Basics of switching (circuit, packet, virtual circuit) and performance metrics
- Data link layer: error detection, Medium Access Control, Ethernet
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs

### Week 14: Computer Networks (Part 2) (Section 10)
- Distance vector and link state routing
- IPv4 — fragmentation, **CIDR notation, Network Address Translation**
- TCP — flow control and congestion control, socket API
- **DNS and HTTP**
- **Practice:** 20 PYQs + 1 topic test

### Week 15: Theory of Computation (Section 6)
- Regular expressions and finite automata
- Context-free grammars and pushdown automata
- Regular and context-free languages, **pumping lemma**
- Turing machines and undecidability
- **Practice:** 25 PYQs

### Week 16: Compiler Design (Section 7) (light coverage — lower weightage)
- Lexical analysis, parsing, syntax-directed translation
- **Runtime environments**
- **Intermediate code generation**
- Local optimisation; **data flow analyses: constant propagation, liveness analysis, common sub-expression elimination**
- **Practice:** 15 PYQs

### Week 17: Math wrap-up + First Full Mock (Section 1)
- Linear Algebra: matrices, determinants, system of linear equations, eigenvalues/eigenvectors, **LU decomposition**
- Calculus: limits, continuity, differentiability, maxima/minima, **Mean Value Theorem**, integration
- Probability & Statistics: random variables, uniform/normal/exponential/Poisson/binomial distributions, mean/median/mode/std. deviation, conditional probability, **Bayes' theorem**
- **Take your first full-length mock test** at the end of this week
- Analyze results — identify your 2-3 weakest subjects

---

## PHASE 5: Revision + Intensive Mocks (Jan 1 – Jan 31) — 4-5 weeks

### Week 18-19: Full Syllabus Revision Round 1
- Revise using your formula/concept notebook, not textbooks
- Solve last 10 years' PYQs subject-wise (fill any gaps)
- 1 full mock every 5-6 days

### Week 20-21: Intensive Mock Phase
- 2-3 full-length mocks per week
- After each mock: spend a full day reviewing every wrong/guessed answer
- Focus extra revision time on your identified weak subjects
- Redo tricky PYQs you got wrong earlier

---

## PHASE 6: Final Sprint (Feb 1 – Exam Day) — ~1-2 weeks

- **No new topics.** Light revision only — formula sheets, short notes
- 1 mock every 2 days max (don't overdo it — avoid burnout)
- Sleep well, keep exam-day logistics ready (admit card, ID, stationery)
- Light daily GA practice to stay sharp

---

## Ongoing throughout (Sept – Feb)
- **General Aptitude:** 15-20 min daily — verbal + quantitative reasoning (worth 15% of total marks, high ROI for prep time)
- **PYQs:** Solve continuously, topic-wise, right after covering each topic — don't save them all for the end
- **Weak-area log:** Note every mistake in mocks/PYQs in one place; revisit weekly

## Recommended resources
- **Digital Logic:** Morris Mano
- **DS/Algorithms:** CLRS (selective), Karumanchi
- **OS:** Galvin
- **DBMS:** Navathe or Korth
- **CN:** Forouzan (selective)
- **TOC:** Ullman or standard GATE notes
- **PYQs & mocks:** GATE Overflow, Made Easy/Ace test series, official GATE PYQ archive

## Weekly time budget (suggested)
- Weekdays: 2-3 hours/day (new topic + practice)
- Weekends: 4-5 hours/day (extra practice + weak topic revision)
- Adjust upward from December onward as mocks intensify
